# Investigation

## Alert Trigger

Microsoft Defender for Identity generated alert **DI‑1001** for **Suspicious lateral movement**. The alert indicated that a user account (jdoe) may have been used to move laterally within the domain, which could signal credential misuse or privilege escalation.

## Triage Steps

1. **Review Local Administrators**  
   - Collected the list of local administrators on the host. Only the expected built‑in accounts and system administrators were present. No unexpected or shadow admin accounts were found.

2. **Review Enabled Local Accounts**  
   - Queried all local user accounts and filtered for enabled accounts. Only legitimate accounts were enabled; there were no unknown or rogue local users.

3. **Inspect Recent Logon Events**  
   - Gathered the ten most recent successful logon (Event ID 4624) events from the Security log. The events showed normal administrative activity and scheduled service logons, with no large volume of failed logon attempts or unusual source IP addresses.

4. **Analyze Running Processes**  
   - Captured the top CPU‑consuming processes at the time of the alert. The list included benign applications such as ChatGPT, Spotify, Chrome, and GoogleDriveFS. No suspicious binaries or privilege‑escalation tools were identified.

## Findings

- The alert was generated by Defender for Identity due to activity matching lateral movement patterns. However, host‑level evidence did not corroborate a compromise.
- No unknown local administrators or enabled accounts were detected.
- Logon events did not reveal brute‑force attempts or anomalous sign‑ins.
- Running processes were consistent with typical user activity.

## Conclusion

Based on the collected evidence, this alert appears to be a **false positive** in a lab environment. There was no sign of compromised credentials or privilege escalation on the investigated host. Nevertheless, the investigation demonstrates the proper workflow for handling Defender for Identity alerts, verifying host integrity, and documenting findings.
